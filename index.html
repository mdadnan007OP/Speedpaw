Quick Guide

What is Download Speed?

Download speed measures how fast information is transferred from the internet to your device. It affects things like loading websites and streaming video.

What is Upload Speed?

Upload speed measures how fast data is sent from your device to the internet. This is important for video calls, sending large files, and gaming.

What is Ping?

Ping (or latency) is the reaction time of your connection—how quickly your device gets a response after you've sent out a request. A fast ping means a more responsive connection.

What is Jitter?

Jitter is the variation in ping over time. High jitter means your ping is fluctuating, which can cause stuttering in voice or video calls and lag in games.

What is Internet Speed?

A comprehensive guide to bandwidth, throughput, and connection speed.

When you subscribe to an internet plan, your provider advertises a number like "100 Mbps" or "1 Gbps". But what does that number actually measure, and why does your internet sometimes feel slow even on a fast plan?

Bandwidth vs. Throughput

To understand internet speed, it helps to imagine a highway. Bandwidth is how many lanes the highway has — the theoretical maximum amount of data that can travel at once. Throughput is how many cars actually make it to their destination without getting stuck in traffic. SpeedPaw measures your actual throughput.

Megabits per Second (Mbps) vs. Megabytes per Second (MB/s)

A common source of confusion is the difference between bits (lowercase b) and bytes (uppercase B):

  • 1 Byte = 8 bits.
  • Network speeds are measured in Megabits per second (Mbps).
  • File sizes on your computer or phone are measured in Megabytes (MB) or Gigabytes (GB).

Quick Rule of Thumb: To estimate download times, divide your connection speed in Mbps by 8. For example, a 100 Mbps connection transfers roughly 12.5 Megabytes per second in ideal conditions.

Download vs. Upload: Why is Upload Slower?

Most residential internet connections (cable, DSL, and some fiber) are asymmetric. They allocate far more bandwidth to download than upload because typical internet usage involves consuming media (streaming, browsing, gaming) rather than hosting servers or uploading giant archives.

What is Ping & Jitter?

Why latency and packet stability matter more than raw speed for gaming and video calls.

While download and upload speed determine how fast large files transfer, ping and jitter determine how responsive your internet feels in real-time interactions.

What is Ping (Latency)?

Ping measures latency: the round-trip time in milliseconds (ms) for a signal to leave your device, reach the speed-test server, and return. Lower ping is always better.

  • < 20 ms: Excellent (ideal for competitive esports and instant web response).
  • 20 – 50 ms: Good (seamless for video calls, gaming, and 4K media).
  • 50 – 100 ms: Acceptable (slight delay in fast-paced multiplayer games).
  • > 100 ms: High latency (noticeable delay in video calls and gaming).

What is Jitter?

Jitter is the variation or inconsistency in ping over time. If one packet takes 20 ms and the next takes 90 ms, your jitter is high. High jitter causes stuttering audio in Zoom or Discord and "rubberbanding" in online games.

Tip: An unstable 500 Mbps Wi-Fi connection with 40 ms jitter can feel far worse than a stable 50 Mbps wired Ethernet cable with 2 ms jitter!

Wi-Fi Optimization & Troubleshooting

How to get the maximum speed out of your wireless home network.

In most homes, the bottleneck is not the internet plan from the ISP — it's the Wi-Fi signal between your router and device.

2.4 GHz vs. 5 GHz vs. 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E/7)

  • 2.4 GHz: Long range and penetrates thick walls easily, but maximum speeds are limited (often 30–60 Mbps in practice) and vulnerable to microwave/Bluetooth interference.
  • 5 GHz: Much faster speeds (often 300–600+ Mbps) and lower latency, but shorter range and struggles through concrete or brick walls.
  • 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E/7): Ultra-fast, pristine spectrum with zero legacy interference, ideal for gigabit connections in the same room.

5 Proven Steps to Boost Wi-Fi Speed

  1. Elevate Your Router: Place the router on a shelf or desk in an open central location, not inside a metal cabinet or on the floor.
  2. Switch to 5 GHz: Connect your primary laptop, smart TV, or console to the 5 GHz band.
  3. Avoid Physical Obstacles: Keep the router away from large mirrors, brick walls, microwaves, and cordless phones.
  4. Restart the Router Monthly: Clears device routing tables and memory leaks.
  5. Use Ethernet for Heavy Devices: A Cat6 Ethernet cable eliminates Wi-Fi jitter completely for gaming and desktop workstations.

Why is My Internet Slow?

A step-by-step checklist to diagnose and fix sluggish internet speeds.

If SpeedPaw shows results significantly lower than your subscribed plan, walk through this quick checklist to find the culprit.

1. Check for Background Downloads

Cloud backups (OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud), game updates (Steam, PlayStation, Xbox), and Windows updates often saturate upload or download bandwidth silently in the background.

2. Test with a Wired Ethernet Cable

To determine if the issue is your Wi-Fi or your ISP line, connect your computer directly to the router via Ethernet and run SpeedPaw. If the speed jumps to your plan's rating, your Wi-Fi configuration is the issue.

3. Check for Peak-Hour Congestion

Between 7 PM and 11 PM, neighborhood cable and broadband lines experience peak demand. If speeds drop consistently every evening, your ISP's local node may be oversubscribed.

4. Inspect Router Age and Cables

Old routers or damaged Ethernet cables (e.g. an old Cat5 cable negotiation capped at 100 Mbps instead of 1 Gbps) can silently cap your entire home network speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about SpeedPaw and internet speed testing.

How does SpeedPaw measure internet speed?

SpeedPaw performs genuine network measurements directly between your browser and our dedicated speed-test server. It pings the server for round-trip latency, downloads non-compressible binary data to calculate real download Mbps, and streams test payload bytes to measure real upload Mbps. No mock values are ever generated.

Why does my speed test result differ from other tests?

Speed tests measure the throughput between your device and a specific server. Factors like physical distance to the server, server load, Wi-Fi interference, and current device background activity will cause slight differences across different testing platforms.

Does SpeedPaw store my personal data or IP address?

No. SpeedPaw is built privacy-first. We do not store your IP address or test results on any server database. Your test history is stored exclusively inside your own browser's local storage (localStorage) and never leaves your device.

Why is my Wi-Fi speed slower than my wired speed?

Wi-Fi signals suffer from atmospheric attenuation, physical obstacles (walls, doors), and radio interference from neighboring networks. An Ethernet cable provides a direct electrical connection with minimal packet loss and zero radio interference.

What is the robot cat mascot?

That's Paw-E, SpeedPaw's friendly robot cat mascot! Paw-E reacts in real time to your network states (curious during server search, alert during ping, and celebrating when your connection is blazing fast).

Privacy Policy

Effective Date: 2026. Last Updated: September 2026.

SpeedPaw values your privacy. This policy outlines how information is handled when you use SpeedPaw.

1. Information We Do Not Collect

SpeedPaw does not require accounts, usernames, or passwords. We do not permanently store your test results or personal profile in any database.

2. Speed Test Measurements

When you conduct a speed test, test bytes are transferred between your browser and our test server to measure throughput. Uploaded test data is held only in volatile memory long enough to tally the byte count and is immediately discarded without being written to persistent storage.

3. Local Storage

Your previous speed test results are saved locally in your browser's localStorage so you can view your personal test history. This data remains on your device and is never sent to our servers. You can clear it at any time with the "Clear All" button.

4. IP Addresses

Your public IP address is used temporarily by the network layer to transmit packets. We do not maintain logs linking IP addresses to individuals.
